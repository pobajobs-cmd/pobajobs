Does it need a liner?

Short answer: not automatically. In England an existing chimney has to be checked and proven for a stove or open fire, not lined by default. Tap any part of the drawing, or its number, to read about it.

The four checksWhat Approved Document J asks for when an existing flue is reused.+
1 · Sweep

Clear soot and tar. Tar from wood can be hard to shift and must be removed.

2 · Look inside

Visual inspection, ideally CCTV. Condition, size, route, damage.

3 · Coring ball

Where there's doubt, drop a ball down to prove the flue is clear.

4 · Smoke test

Seal top and bottom, fill with smoke, look for leaks anywhere in the house.

Passes

Use it as it is, if the size suits the appliance. No liner is required by the regulations, unless the stove maker asks for one.

Fails

Don't use it until it's put right: repair the stack or reline it with one of the solid-fuel linings below. Relining is notifiable work.

Source: Approved Document J paras 1.35–1.37, Appendix E3 · SFA 2026

Types of linerClay and steel aren't the only options. Here's every common lining, and the one never to use.+
Clay / ceramic linersBS EN 1457 · rigid

Sections built in, socket end up. Standard in chimneys built since 1965, and can last the life of the building. Relining means opening up the chimney, and the 20–25 mm walls shrink the flue.

Pumice / concrete linersBS EN 1857 · rigid

Rigid sections or blocks. Pumice insulates well, so it's recommended for wood stoves. Installed the same way as clay.

Pumped refractory concreteCast in-situ · ADJ 2.20

An inflatable former goes down the flue and lightweight concrete is pumped round it. Fills cracks and strengthens the stack. No British Standard, so the result depends on the installer.

Flexible steel (twin-skin)BS EN 1856-2 · 904L or 316Ti

Double-skinned, smooth inside, fitted the right way up. For relining only (ADJ 1.40). Backfill with perlite or vermiculite for wood stoves, never fibreglass. Sweep with a poly brush.

Spray-on liningAbout 7 mm · specialist

A spray head is drawn up the cleaned flue. Thin, so it keeps the most flue size, and it follows bends. Good for cracked clay liners. Make sure the guarantee is insurance-backed.

Ceramic coatingAbout 4 mm · specialist

A plug is pulled up the flue, pressing liquid lining into the walls and sealing cracks. Barely reduces the flue size. Usually guaranteed for ten years.

Twin-wall system chimneyBS EN 1856-1 · G50 / G60 typical

Not a liner: a complete insulated chimney for homes with none. Keep its rated gap to combustibles. See Clearances below.

Single-skin flexi (gas/oil)Never for solid fuel or wood

Light-gauge liner for gas fires, boilers and oil. It isn't built for a stove or open fire, whatever the price.

Sources: SFA guide to lining old chimneys 2026 · Approved Document J paras 1.27, 1.39–1.40, 2.20, Table 3 · BFCMA guide

ClearancesHow far combustibles must be from a chimney, a stove pipe and a twin-wall flue.+
Masonry chimney
  • Combustibles at least 200 mm from the inside of the flue.
  • 40 mm from the outside face (floorboards and skirting are excepted).
  • Metal fixings 50 mm from the inside of the flue.
Single-wall stove pipe
  • At least 3 × its diameter from combustibles: 450 mm for a 150 mm pipe.
  • Down to 1.5 × D behind a non-combustible shield that extends 1.5 × D past the pipe, with a 12 mm air gap behind it.
  • It only links the stove to the chimney. It mustn't pass through roof spaces, partitions, internal walls or floors (ADJ 2.14).
Twin-wall flue
  • Keep the maker's rated gap, printed in its designation: G50 means 50 mm. G50 and G60 are typical.
  • Through floors and ceilings, use the maker's fire-stop or support plates to hold that gap. Use fire-rated parts or casing through compartment floors (ADJ 1.46).
  • Box it in with a guard through cupboards, storage and roof spaces (1.45). Sleeve it through walls, and no hidden joints without access (1.43–1.44).

Sources: Approved Document J paras 1.42–1.46, 2.14–2.15, 2.18, Diagram 19 · LABC stove guide · Twin-wall boxing

The Clean Air ActIt controls smoke and fuel, not how chimneys are built. Here's what it does cover.+
1952
The Great Smog

Five days of coal smoke over London. About 4,000 extra deaths were counted at the time; later estimates go as high as 12,000.

1956
Clean Air Act

Councils can declare smoke control areas, and grants help people convert their fireplaces.

1965
Building Regs, not the CAA

New chimneys must be built with liners. This is the date that matters for lining.

1968
Clean Air Act

Dark smoke from a chimney becomes an offence.

1993
Clean Air Act

Replaces the 1956 and 1968 Acts. Authorised fuels and exempt appliances are listed under it.

2021
Wet wood rules

From May, wood sold in amounts under 2 m³ must be Ready to Burn: 20% moisture or less.

2022
Smoke penalties

From May, £175–£300 civil penalties for chimney smoke in a smoke control area. The old "exempt appliance" defence goes.

2023
House coal

From May, traditional house coal can no longer be sold in England.

2026
Consultation

In January, Defra opened a UK-wide consultation on further limits. No decision yet.

No smoke

In a smoke control area, smoke can't come from your chimney. Councils can fine the occupier £175–£300.

Fuel or stove

Burn authorised smokeless fuel, or use a Defra-exempt appliance with the fuels it's approved for. Open fires: smokeless fuel only.

Buying it

Buying or selling unauthorised fuel for a non-exempt appliance in a smoke control area is illegal.

Why it matters for lining: "pre-Clean Air Act" doesn't mean a chimney is non-compliant. The Act is about what comes out of a chimney. How a chimney is built and tested is covered by Building Regulations Part J. Check whether a home is in a smoke control area on GOV.UK or with the council.

Sources: GLA, 70 years since the smog · London Museum · Clean Air Act 1993 · Domestic solid fuels rules · Smoke control enforcement · 2026 consultation

MythsThe things people say about liners, and what the guidance actually says.+
"The regs say every stove must be lined."

They don't. There's no automatic requirement to line an existing chimney. It has to be suitable for the appliance and fuel.

"Pre-Clean Air Act chimneys must be lined."

The Clean Air Act is about smoke. Liners for new chimneys came in with the 1965 Building Regulations. Old chimneys are judged on the tests.

"HETAS requires a liner."

HETAS installers work to Approved Document J. The stove maker's instructions can require a liner, and those must be followed.

"Any flexi liner will do."

Solid fuel needs a twin-skin liner (904L or 316Ti). Single-skin gas liners must never be used with a stove or open fire.
