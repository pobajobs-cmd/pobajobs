Geominals guide

Three workspaces, picked from the top bar:

ArtTurn a picture into geometric art - prints, T-shirts, laser-cut SVGs, 3D plaques, mockups and videos.
MapsReal elevation for any place - stacked laser layers with an instruction booklet, or a 3D print.
BinsGridfinity bins shaped to your tools from a photo, plus storage bins, hole holders, baseplates and router templates.

Where things are saved

Everything you make is saved on the PC in D:\Programs\Geominals\output, in a folder named after the design, map or bin. Each result shows its files as links, and Download all (.zip) gets the lot - handy from a phone.

Good to know

  • Hover over any setting (or long-press on a phone) for a short explanation.
  • Settings are grouped in panels you can fold away - click a group's title to open or close it. The app remembers which you left open.
  • Slow jobs (maps, bins, scans) run in the background: you can switch workspace and come back, and Cancel stops them.
  • 3D results open in a viewer: drag to turn, scroll or pinch to zoom, double-click to reset, ⤢ for full screen.

Art

  1. Open image (or drop one on the canvas). A PNG with a transparent background is best; for photos use Cut out to remove the background (AI works on any background, Quick on plain ones).
  2. Pick a style for the layer on the right, or a ready-made combination from Mixes.
  3. Fine-tune: the right panel edits the selected layer, the left panel the whole picture.
  4. Export when you're happy.

Shading & layers

  • Shading from (left panel) decides where detail goes. Image tone puts more geometry where the picture is dark. Depth uses a small local AI model so near parts get the most detail. Shape ignores the photo and shades the silhouette like a rounded 3D form.
  • The Tone map view (top of the canvas) shows exactly what the styles see: dark = dense.
  • Layers stack like in any image editor. Each can cover the whole subject or a Region: a tone range (e.g. only the dark parts), an inset, or a band round the edge - that's how mixes put one style in the shadows and another in the highlights.
  • Styles are grouped: Triangles, Cells, Lines, Ornament, Patterns, Art and Plotter (one-line drawing, string art, flow lines… good for pen plotters and laser engraving).
  • Recipe ▾ saves all your settings to a file and loads them back, so you can repeat a look on another picture.
  • Output (left): Print for transparent PNG / SVG artwork, Laser for cut-and-score colours. Set the finished size in mm.

Maps

  1. Choose the place: type a place name, coordinates or paste a Google Maps link and press Find. Click the map to move the centre. The dashed outline is the area; its dashed line shows "up".
  2. Settings: set the width in km, the shape (square, rectangle, circle, hexagon, heart) and which way is up (Top edge faces, 0 = north - for a table, the direction you face when sitting at it).
  3. Press Preview for a quick look, then Laser layers or 3D print.
  • Sea floor shades the sea in blues with real depths (very detailed round Europe).
  • Map features add roads, rivers & lakes, railways and buildings from OpenStreetMap, plus a centre marker.
  • Border, label & compass: a frame, a plate with the name, coordinates and your own text, a scale bar and a compass rose.
  • Use in Art sends the terrain to the Art workspace - the Terrain style then draws true contour lines.
  • Credit "© OpenStreetMap contributors" on anything you sell that uses map features or place names.

Laser layers

  • Set the sheet thickness and number of layers. Each layer is one sheet of material.
  • Every piece can be numbered, and where the next layer sits is scored (slightly inset so it hides under the glue-up).
  • Nest onto sheet W × H packs every piece onto sheets of your material size. Pieces bigger than the sheet are split, with seams hidden under the next layer and staggered like brickwork.
  • Hollow layers cut away the hidden middle of big pieces (with ribs so nothing sags) to save material - smaller pieces get nested into the openings.
  • The result viewer has Layers, Sheets and 3D tabs. 3D shows the finished stack to scale - switch between Colours, Wood and Stained wood.
  • An instructions PDF shows every layer with its numbered pieces, to put it back together.

3D prints

  • Each colour band is its own part in the 3MF, so an AMS can print it in colours. Sea, roads, rivers, rails and buildings are separate parts too.
  • Border can be level with the highest point or follow the edge terrain. Set Printer bed W/H to split big maps into tiles.

Scanning tools

Outline · 1 photo - quick and accurate for flat tools:

  1. Lay the tools flat on white paper with a little space between them and away from the edges. The printable scan sheet is best (print at 100%, check the 100 mm bar with a ruler); any plain A4/A3/Letter or a custom size works if all four corners show.
  2. Take one photo from straight above, in even light, no flash glare. A darker table under the paper helps.
  3. Press Find tools. With Check paper corners on, the photo opens with the paper's corners marked - drag any that are off onto the real corners (a magnifier appears while you drag). This sets the scale and straightens the photo.
  4. For each tool found: give it a name and its depth (thickness), leave Correct for parallax on, check the outline with Edit outline if needed, then Save.

Parallax: a thick tool's top is closer to the camera, so it looks bigger than it is. The app works out where the camera was from the paper corners and takes that back off using the depth you type. Taking the photo from higher up (zoom in a little) reduces it further.

3D · several photos - for tools with a shaped underside (the pocket floor follows it):

  1. Print the scan sheet at 100% - 3D needs its markers. Lay the tools upside down in the blank middle.
  2. One photo from above, then 6-8 walking round the sheet at about 25-35° above the table, with all the markers in every photo.
  3. Thin tools come out a little deep - give Max thickness (measured with calipers) to cap it.

Other ways: By size for rectangles, round things and hex bits; From a picture for a flatbed scan (give the DPI - the most accurate outline there is) or a silhouette / transparent PNG (give the real length).

Editing outlines

Every found tool and every saved tool has Edit outline, a path editor like Photoshop's:

Move a pointDrag it. A magnifier shows exactly where it is.
Add pointsTurn on Add points (or press A), then click or tap an edge where you want one.
Remove a pointDouble-click / double-tap it, or select it and press Delete or Delete point.
HolesAdd shape puts a square in the middle - inside another shape it's a hole. Delete shape removes the selected one (e.g. a false hole).
Zoom / panScroll or pinch to zoom, drag the background to pan, Fit to see it all.
PointsMore points follow the edge closely; fewer are quicker to tidy. Changing it redraws the outline.
UndoUndo or Ctrl+Z. Cancel throws the edit away.

Tool bins

  1. In the library press Put in bin → (set how many first). Tools are auto-arranged into the smallest bin that fits your printer bed.
  2. Drag tools to move them. Select one to rotate (or press R), mirror, change its finger scoop, pocket depth, pocket shape (exact, smoothed, convex, rectangle), extra clearance or the name shown in its pocket. Arrow keys nudge, Ctrl+Z undoes.
  3. Red means a tool hits the wall or is too close to another. Auto arrange and Centre tidy up. If it can't all fit, you'll be offered to save this bin and start the next with the rest.
  4. Make bin. The result shows in 3D with the files, filament and cost estimate.
  • Height: auto (just deep enough), standard 3 units, double 6, triple 9, quad 12, or custom. 1 unit = 7 mm.
  • Hollows for loose bits dig the leftover space into rounded trays, optionally divided every N mm.
  • Tools stand proud leaves them sticking up to grab; bevel rounds pocket edges.
  • Two colours (AMS): coloured pocket floors (a bright floor shows a missing tool) and coloured text. Load the 3MF as one object with two parts.
  • Pickup tab + label holder on the back, with a printed label insert. Nothing can stack on a tabbed bin.
  • Lid, magnet holes (6 × 2 mm), M3 screw holes, stacking lip.
  • Check first: the 1:1 paper template PDF to lay the tools on, and a 1.6 mm fit-test print of just the outlines.
  • Layouts are saved - Open a saved bin… brings one back.

Storage bins

Set the size and how many compartments across and front to back. Suggest the smallest bin works it out from how many compartments you need and the smallest each must be. Options: scoops at the front, angled label tabs with engraved labels, lower dividers, lid, pickup tab.

Hole holders

List the sizes - 1, 1.5, 3mm, 1/8in, 1-1/2in, M6 - or pick a preset (metric or imperial drills, ¼" hex bits, allen keys). Each gets a round or hex hole with fit added (0.3 mm drops in loosely, 0.15 is snug), deeper for bigger sizes, and its size engraved in front. Rows step up towards the back so every label shows. The plan updates as you type.

Baseplates

Give the drawer's inside size to fill it (whole cells, centred, padding to the walls) or a number of cells. Light uses the least plastic; Solid, Magnet and Screw (countersunk holes to fix it in the drawer) are sturdier. Big plates are split into tiles for your bed, joined by printed dog-bone clips underneath (solid styles).

Router templates

  • Make one from the current tool-bin layout or a single tool (Router template on a library card).
  • Internal: a plate with a hole per tool - the bit cuts the pockets. External: shaped pieces to run round the outside of.
  • Bearing (flush-trim / pattern bit): the template is the exact shape. Guide bushing: the template is offset by (bushing − bit) ÷ 2 - only use it with that bushing and bit.
  • Corners tighter than the bit are widened just enough that the tools fit (red on the preview); anything still out of reach is flagged.
  • Files: SVG and DXF for the laser (red cut, blue engraved notes), a smooth STL to 3D print (6 mm+ thick so the bearing rides on it), a 1:1 PDF to stick on hardboard, and the depth to route for each tool.
  • Foam: clearance 0-0.5 mm. Stick the template down with double-sided tape; glue any loose islands (inside tool rings) in place.

Printing tips

  • 0.2 mm layers, no supports. The feet, lip and baseplate follow the standard Gridfinity profile, so they mix with anyone's bins.
  • If bins are tight in the baseplate, check your printer's flow / dimensional calibration before changing designs.
  • Magnets: 6 × 2 mm discs, a dab of glue.

CNC router

The SVG, DXF and STL files work in any CAM program (Estlcam, Carbide Create, Kiri:Moto, Fusion…). Geominals can also write ready-to-run G-code itself.

Map: Laser layersTick Also write CNC G-code (Maps → Settings → CNC router). For each sheet (or each layer, without a sheet size) you get _0_engrave.nc (score lines, numbers, features) and _1_cut.nc (every piece cut out with tabs, holes cut first). The gap between pieces is raised so the bit fits.
Map: 3D printThe whole map carved from one block: 1_rough (flat end mill, in levels, leaving a little), 2_finish (ball nose, parallel passes) and 3_cutout (cuts the shape free with tabs). Set the stock thickness, or 0 to use the model's height.
Bins: Router & CNCTick CNC G-code: pocket the foam directly - each tool's pocket to its own depth, no template needed. Give a stock thickness to cut the insert out too.

Before the first job

  • Open Machine settings… once: controller (GRBL / FluidNC, or Marlin on V1 Engineering boards), how the router is switched on, your bits, feeds and depth per pass. Every CNC file uses them.
  • Zero: X/Y on the stock's bottom-left corner, Z on its top. Re-zero Z after every bit change (rough → finish).
  • Check each file's toolpath picture (_cnc.png) and do an air cut (Z zeroed well above the stock) the first time.
  • Only straight moves are used, so every controller runs them; a G-code viewer such as NC Viewer shows them in 3D.
  • Pieces smaller than about three bit widths can break or fly - they're flagged. Inside corners come out rounded to the bit's radius; that's normal for a router.

Phone

  • Start Geominals with Start Geominals (phone).bat (the TV PC does this automatically), then press Phone on the PC and scan the QR code. Same Wi-Fi as the PC.
  • The phone does everything the PC does - great for taking tool photos straight into Bins with Take photo.
  • Away from home: install Tailscale on the PC and phone and the same link works anywhere.

Troubleshooting

No tools found / blobby outlinesMore contrast (dark tools on white), even light without glare, raise Sensitivity for pale tools, lower it if shadows are caught. Then tidy with Edit outline.
Sizes slightly offCheck the paper corners are right on the corners, the paper size is correct, and (for the scan sheet) it printed at 100%. Type each tool's depth so parallax is corrected.
3D scan too thickMore side photos at lower angles, and set Max thickness.
Map fails to downloadOpenStreetMap servers get busy - try again in a minute; elevation is cached so retries are quick. Turn features off to make the map without them.
Phone can't connectSame Wi-Fi as the PC, started with the phone .bat, and VPNs off on the phone.
Something looks stuckPress Cancel. If the page itself misbehaves, refresh it - running jobs carry on in the background. Closing and reopening Geominals restarts it fully.

CNC machine

Used for every CNC file Geominals writes. Plain G-code (straight moves only), origin at the stock's bottom-left corner, Z0 on its top.
